Two kinds of "price per kg"

Deadweight is the price per kg of carcase, paid by the abattoir for finished cattle.

Liveweight is the price per kg of the live animal, paid at auction for stores and calves.

A carcase weighs much less than the live animal, so a deadweight price per kg is always much higher than a liveweight one. Don't compare the two directly. Compare the price per head instead.

What these words mean

Deadweight
Price per kilo of the carcase after slaughter, paid by the abattoir.
Liveweight
Price per kilo of the live animal, as sold at auction.
Stores
Cattle sold before they are ready for slaughter, for another farm to finish.
p/kg
Pence per kilo.
Grade (for example R4L)
How a carcase is graded. The letter is the shape (from best to poorest: E, U, R, O, P). The number is the fat cover (1 is leanest, 5 is fattest, with 4 split into 4L lower and 4H higher).
O+
The upper half of the O shape grade, one step below R.
−O
The lower half of the O shape grade, one step below O+.
Prime cattle
Young cattle finished for slaughter: steers, heifers and young bulls (not cows).
AHDB
The Agriculture and Horticulture Development Board, the farming levy body. It collects prices from abattoirs every week.
Before deductions
The price before costs such as haulage, levy and insurance are taken off.

Steers and heifers only. Cows and young bulls are not included.

All prices are market averages. What any one animal makes depends on its weight, grade, buyer and the deductions taken off, so your own prices will differ. This site is for information only. It is not advice on when to buy or sell.
